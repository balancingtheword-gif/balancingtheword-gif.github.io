The Royal Priesthood

God’s Kingdom of Priests

By Lesli D. Mize

Published July 22, 2026

Discover how Israel’s priesthood established a biblical foundation for understanding the calling, holiness, and service of God’s people.

God’s Original Design

From the very beginning, God's intention for His people has never been passive faith, but active priesthood. At Mount Sinai, the Lord declared His purpose plainly: "You shall be to Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" (Exodus 19:6). This was not merely a promise for Israel in the wilderness—it was God's divine blueprint for a people set apart to serve Him.

Centuries later, through the prophet Joel, God promised a day when His Spirit would no longer rest upon a select few but would be poured out upon all flesh (Joel 2:28). That long-awaited moment arrived on the Day of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit came with power, filling ordinary men and women for God's service (Acts 2:1–4). What began as a promise became a commissioning.

Peter, standing on the other side of that outpouring, declared what is now true of every believer in Christ: "You are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for God's own possession" (1 Peter 2:9). This declaration reveals the biblical meaning of the royal priesthood. It is not poetic language—it is our identity. We are not spectators in God's kingdom but consecrated servants, Spirit-filled ambassadors, and living vessels of His glory.

This identity carries both privilege and responsibility. Before we can fully understand the royal priesthood and the priesthood of all believers under the New Covenant, we must first understand the Old Testament priesthood that God established for Israel. By examining the calling, responsibilities, and ministry of Israel's priests, we lay the biblical foundation for understanding how these principles find their fulfillment in Christ.

The Priestly Roles

Mediators Between God and Humanity

According to Scripture, the priests of the Old Covenant were appointed by God to serve as mediators between Him and the people of Israel. As the writer of Hebrews explains, "Every high priest taken from among men is appointed for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins" (Hebrews 5:1). Their ministry centered on representing the people before God through sacrifices, intercession, and worship while representing God before the people by teaching His Law and declaring His commands. In this way, the priesthood served as a sacred bridge between a holy God and a sinful nation.

Under the Old Covenant, this sacred responsibility was entrusted exclusively to the tribe of Levi, and more specifically to the descendants of Aaron, whom God called, consecrated, and appointed to serve as priests (Exodus 28:1; Numbers 3:10). Their ministry was carefully governed by God's instructions concerning sacrifices, ceremonial cleansing, holy conduct, and tabernacle—later temple—service (Exodus 28–29; Leviticus 8–9). Every aspect of their ministry reflected God's holiness and reminded Israel that sinful people could approach a holy God only according to His appointed way.

The priesthood was never a position of personal ambition or self-appointment. Only those whom God called and set apart could minister before Him. Yet God's calling was only the beginning. Before a priest could enter into his sacred duties, he had to be consecrated and prepared according to God's instructions.

Holiness and Consecration

The priests were to be holy—that is, set apart for God's service. Their lives were to reflect the purity of God, marked by conduct, devotion, and obedience that distinguished them from the rest of the people.

They were also to be consecrated through a deliberate and sacred process. This consecration included a specific washing, as outlined in Exodus 29, symbolizing purification and preparation before they could minister before the Lord.

Consecration, however, involved far more than ceremonial washing. While the outward rituals symbolized purification, God was equally concerned with the inward character of those who served before Him. Priestly ministry required not only hands that had been cleansed for service, but hearts that reflected God's holiness, compassion, and righteousness.

Moral Integrity and Compassion

Because their responsibility was to represent the people before God, priests were required to possess strong moral integrity. Because their responsibility was to minister before a holy God, priests were required to maintain moral integrity and ceremonial purity. The Law also established physical qualifications for those serving at the altar, emphasizing the holiness required in God's presence (Leviticus 21:16–24). They were also to possess compassion, mercy, grace, and a deep sense of justice. If they were removed from human struggle and untouched by human weakness, they could not effectively intercede on behalf of others. Scripture reveals this truth most clearly in Jesus Himself, who, as Hebrews 5:2 teaches, was able to deal gently with humanity because He willingly shared in our human weakness.

A priest's holy character was never an end in itself. God set His priests apart so they could faithfully carry out the sacred work entrusted to them. One of their highest responsibilities was to stand before God on behalf of others, bearing the sins and needs of the people through intercession and sacrifice.

Intercession

The Old Testament priests served as intercessors, standing before God on behalf of the people. Intercession meant standing in the gap—positioning themselves between a holy God and a sinful nation, pleading for His mercy and seeking reconciliation on behalf of the people.

One of the clearest examples of this ministry is found during Korah's rebellion. When God's judgment broke out among the Israelites, Moses instructed Aaron to take his censer, place incense in it, and run into the midst of the congregation to make atonement for the people. Scripture records that "he stood between the dead and the living, so that the plague was checked" (Numbers 16:48). Aaron's actions vividly illustrated the priest's calling to stand in the gap between God's righteous judgment and a people in need of His mercy.

Intercession was one of the priest's highest privileges and responsibilities. By continually bringing the needs, sins, and burdens of the people before the Lord, the priest reminded Israel that reconciliation with God required His mercy and His appointed means of approach.

Sacrifice

Closely connected to the ministry of intercession was the ministry of sacrifice. Under the Old Covenant, the priests offered the sacrifices prescribed by God as means of atonement for sin. These offerings were not human inventions but acts of worship established by God Himself, demonstrating both the seriousness of sin and the necessity of atonement.

Every sacrifice pointed to God's holiness, His justice, and His mercy. Through the continual offering of burnt offerings, sin offerings, guilt offerings, and peace offerings, the priests reminded the nation that sin separated humanity from God, and that forgiveness could only be obtained according to His appointed way.

The sacrificial system continually reinforced the cost of sin while revealing God's gracious provision for restoring fellowship with Himself.

Teaching and Guardians of Truth

The priest's ministry extended beyond the altar. While he represented the people before God through intercession and sacrifice, he also represented God before the people by faithfully declaring His truth.

The Old Testament priests were not merely ritual performers; they were also teachers of the Law. They served as guardians of truth and knowledge, entrusted by God to preserve His Word, teach the Mosaic Law, and instruct the people in His ways.

Malachi 2:7 clearly defines God's expectation of the priesthood:

"For the lips of a priest should preserve knowledge, and men should seek instruction from his mouth; for he is the messenger of the LORD of hosts."

Reconciliation with God required more than sacrifices; it required knowledge of God's Word and a willingness to walk in obedience. For this reason, the Lord entrusted the priests not only with sacred ceremonies but also with the responsibility of faithfully teaching His truth and leading His people in covenant faithfulness.

Discernment: Distinguishing the Holy from the Common

Teaching God's truth required more than knowledge of the Law; it required spiritual discernment. The priests were entrusted with the responsibility of distinguishing between what was holy and what was common, and between what was clean and what was unclean. The Lord commanded Aaron and his sons, "You are to distinguish between the holy and the common, and between the unclean and the clean" (Leviticus 10:10). Likewise, through the prophet Ezekiel, God declared, "They shall teach My people the difference between the holy and the common, and show them how to distinguish between the unclean and the clean" (Ezekiel 44:23).

This responsibility extended far beyond ceremonial regulations. The priests were called to exercise sound spiritual judgment, preserving God's standards and helping His people recognize the difference between truth and error, righteousness and sin, obedience and compromise. Their discernment protected the holiness of God's worship and guided the nation in faithful covenant living.

By faithfully teaching God's Word and exercising righteous discernment, the priests guarded the spiritual health of Israel. They reminded the people that God's holiness was never to be treated as ordinary.

The Priesthood

More Than an Ancient Office

Although the Old Testament priesthood belonged to a specific time under the Mosaic Covenant, the principles that governed it reveal timeless truths about God's character and His expectations for those who serve Him. The priest's calling to holiness, faithful instruction, compassionate intercession, and devoted service reflected God's own character and His desire for His people to know Him.

As we have seen, the Old Testament priest was called by God, consecrated for His service, entrusted with the ministry of intercession, and charged with faithfully teaching God's truth. These responsibilities formed the foundation of the priesthood throughout Israel's history.

Yet, the story of the priesthood does not end there.

With the coming of Jesus Christ, God accomplished something extraordinary. The work of the priesthood was not abolished—it was fulfilled and expanded in ways the Old Testament only foreshadowed. The New Testament reveals that these priestly principles now carry profound significance for every follower of Christ.
