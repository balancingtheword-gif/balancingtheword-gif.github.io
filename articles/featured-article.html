Revival Begins in the Heart

How the Light of Christ Transforms Us

By Lesli D. Mize

Published August 19, 2026

Discover how true biblical revival begins when the Light of Christ exposes darkness, leads us to repentance, and transforms a surrendered heart.

Read-Along Podcast

Listen as Lesli D. Mize narrates this featured message.

The Illusion of Revival

Living in a state known for its strong Christian identity, we have churches representing every kind of doctrine lining the streets. It is commonplace to see Bible verses on signs, and revival services are regularly held and widely promoted. So, one might assume that true spiritual revival and renewal are flourishing. Invitations to these revivals promise encounters with God, freedom from bondage, healing for the wounded, and breakthroughs for the weary. The music swells, emotions rise, and altars are filled. Yet when the revival ends and the excitement fades, how many lives experience the lasting spiritual transformation that true biblical revival produces?

An emotional experience can move the heart without transforming a life. A powerful atmosphere may stir people for a moment, but if there is no call to repentance, no proclamation of salvation through Jesus Christ, and no surrender to the authority of God’s Word, can it truly be called revival?

True revival is not found merely in an event, a crowded sanctuary, or a musical crescendo. It cannot be manufactured through excitement or sustained by temporary inspiration. Authentic revival begins in the heart—when pride is broken, sin is confronted and confessed, and a life is fully surrendered to Christ.

Revival is more than feeling something in God’s presence; it is responding to His presence. It is allowing the Holy Spirit to expose what is hidden, awaken what has grown cold, and restore what has drifted away from God. It produces more than a moment at the altar—it produces obedience, holiness, spiritual hunger, and lasting change.

Only through Christ can true freedom take root. Only through surrender can lives be transformed. Only when His Light is welcomed into every hidden place can the darkness be driven out.

The question, then, is not whether we can create the appearance of revival, but whether we are willing to receive the One from whom true revival comes. If revival is the awakening of hearts once held in darkness, we must turn our attention to Jesus Christ—the only Light powerful enough to expose sin, lead us to repentance, and transform our lives.

Christ: The True Light

Centuries before the birth of Christ, Isaiah prophesied that a Great Light would dawn upon those living in deep spiritual darkness (Isaiah 9:1–2). Matthew declared that this promise was fulfilled when Jesus began His earthly ministry in Galilee (Matthew 4:15–16). His message was direct and urgent: “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand” (Matthew 4:17).

Jesus did not come merely to improve people’s circumstances or provide temporary relief from their suffering. He came to rescue humanity from the power of sin and lead those walking in darkness into the Light of eternal life. Throughout Scripture, we see who Jesus is and what His coming means for those who believe in Him.

Therefore, Jesus is:

  1. The Light of the world—the One who delivers His followers from darkness and gives them the Light of life (John 8:12).

  2. The true Light—the One who came into the world to bring divine truth and revelation to all humanity (John 1:9).

  3. The Light darkness cannot overcome—the One whose power can neither be extinguished nor defeated (John 1:5).

John the Baptist: A Witness to the Light

Before the true Light was fully revealed to the world, God raised up John the Baptist as a messenger to prepare the way. John did not come to draw attention to himself or establish his own following. He came to awaken hearts, call people to repentance, and direct them toward the coming Messiah.

Jesus described John as a burning and shining lamp because he faithfully carried the light he had been given (John 5:35). Yet John was not the source of the Light; he was a witness who pointed others toward it. A lamp carries light, but Christ is the Light. John’s ministry was temporary, but Christ is the greater, eternal, and abiding Light who continues to shine.

Through His suffering, death, and resurrection, Jesus fulfilled God’s promise to proclaim Light to both the Jewish people and the Gentiles (Acts 26:23). At the cross, darkness appeared to triumph—but through the resurrection, Christ demonstrated that neither sin, death, nor the powers of darkness could overcome Him.

Where the Light of Christ shines, sin is exposed, truth is revealed, and hearts are called to repentance. This is why genuine revival cannot be separated from the message Jesus proclaimed. Where Christ moves, repentance precedes revival.

Christ’s Light does more than shine before us—it transforms us. His Light reveals truth, delivers us from darkness, and calls us to a new way of living.

What the Light Does

The Light of Christ is not merely descriptive—it is transformative. It does not simply reveal who Jesus is; it reshapes who we are. As His Light works within us, it accomplishes three essential things:

  1. It guides and leads us—The Light of Christ directs our steps, offering wisdom and clarity so we can walk in truth and avoid spiritual darkness (John 8:12; Psalm 119:105).

  2. It reveals the glory of God—Through His Light, we come to see God’s character, beauty, and presence more clearly, deepening our reverence and understanding of Him (2 Corinthians 4:6).

  3. It gives us a new identity—As the Light transforms us, our past brokenness no longer defines us, but by who we are becoming in Christ (2 Corinthians 5:17; Ephesians 5:8).

The Light does not leave us as it found us. It reshapes our character, redirects our steps, and transforms how we live. Yet the work of the Light does not end with our personal transformation. Christ does not bring us out of darkness merely for our own benefit; He calls us to carry His truth into a world that remains spiritually blind. Once His Light has changed us, it must also shine through us.

The Light at Work Through Us

The Light that transforms us also commissions us. When Christ called Paul, He sent him to open spiritually blind eyes and turn people from darkness to Light and from the power of Satan to God (Acts 26:18). Through faith in Christ, they could receive forgiveness of sins and an inheritance among those who are sanctified.

Through the proclamation of the gospel, Christ works to: open spiritually blind eyes so people can recognize the truth, turn people from darkness to Light by leading them out of deception and into the truth of Christ, deliver them from Satan’s dominion and bring them under the authority of God, offer forgiveness of sins through repentance and faith in Jesus Christ, and provide an eternal inheritance among all who are sanctified by faith in Him.

Paul could not accomplish these things through his own power. His responsibility was to proclaim the truth faithfully; Christ was the One who opened eyes, broke spiritual bondage, forgave sin, and transformed lives.

Paul’s commission reflected the continuing mission of Christ—a mission now entrusted to all who carry the gospel. We are not the source of the Light, nor do we possess the power to save. Like John the Baptist and the apostle Paul, we are witnesses who point others toward Jesus. The Light belongs to Christ, but He chooses to let it shine through us.

This calling was not limited to Paul, the apostles, or those serving in recognized ministry positions. Every believer who has received the Light of Christ is called to reflect that Light before others. We may not all preach from a pulpit or travel to distant nations, but our words, choices, conduct, and treatment of others should reveal the presence of Christ within us.

Disciples Are the Light

In Matthew 5:13–16, Jesus calls His followers the salt of the earth and the light of the world. We are not called to disappear into the surrounding culture or conceal the truth we have received. Like a city set on a hill that cannot be hidden, our lives should make Christ visible to those around us.

Imagine a lost child wandering through dark woods who suddenly sees the light of a distant house. That light becomes a beacon of hope, providing direction and promising safety. Likewise, a believer who reflects Christ can guide others from spiritual darkness toward hope in Jesus Christ.

Not everyone will welcome the Light. Some will be drawn to it because they are searching for truth, while others will resist it because it exposes what they would rather keep hidden. Nevertheless, we do not dim our light to gain acceptance, nor do we shine to receive recognition or praise.

We shine because we reflect Christ. Our good works are not intended to draw attention to ourselves but to direct others toward our heavenly Father. When His character is visible in the way we live, serve, speak, and love, others are given an opportunity to see His goodness and glorify Him.

Go Deeper

Revival begins when the Light of Christ transforms a surrendered heart, but it does not end there. Those who receive His Light are called to live as illuminated witnesses—believing His truth, guarding what enters their hearts, walking in obedience, loving others, proclaiming the gospel boldly, and refusing to allow fear or compromise to conceal their light.

Scripture also warns us that sound doctrine, good works, and outward perseverance are not enough when our love for Christ has grown cold. To remain in the Light, we must continue in repentance, holiness, truth, and genuine love. Like Paul and Barnabas, we are called to carry the gospel forward even when we encounter resistance, trusting God to turn rejection into redirection and opposition into an opportunity for the Light to advance.

Revival is not merely an event we attend or an experience we wait to receive. True revival becomes visible when transformed believers carry the Light of Christ into a darkened world.

The question is not simply, “When will revival come?”

The question is, “Will I allow His revival to begin—and continue—through me?”

The Challenge

This week, ask yourself: Is the Light of Christ producing visible change in my life, or have I mistaken an emotional experience for true revival? Prayerfully identify one area that remains hidden in darkness, surrender it to Christ, and take one deliberate step of obedience. Let true revival begin within you—and allow your transformed life to shine before others.

Prayer

Heavenly Father,

Thank You for sending Jesus, the Light of the world, to lead us out of darkness. Let Your Light expose anything within us that does not honor You, renew our minds, and guide our steps. Transform us into faithful reflections of Christ so that others may see Your truth and glory shining through our lives.

In Jesus’ name, amen.
